Four directions

Four different philosophies, not four shades of one. Each uses only style-guide colours, so the five-navies problem is fixed in all of them — the sidebar is Midnight Blue everywhere, that's a given rather than a variable. Each also carries exactly one Caveat line, per the one-scribble-per-page rule. Real nav labels, real logo files. Nothing here is implemented.

1

Peers

The two paths become genuine equals. Both sit on light grounds and are told apart by their accent — Shamrock for Request, warm gold for Deals — instead of one being dark and heavy and the other pale. Settles the hierarchy the copy already promises ("Two ways to go from here") rather than contradicting it.

The opposite decision, made deliberately. Request is the product — so it becomes a full-width Midnight Blue hero with the video band running across it, and Deals sits beneath as a confident but secondary strip. Nothing floats; the composition fills the screen top to bottom.

Nothing here is implemented — these are concepts in a standalone file, and neither dashboard has been touched since the brand pass went live.

If you want my read: 3 is the one that makes the brand unmistakable and it's the closest to what your own style guide shows on its typography page — but 4 is the one that makes the page worth returning to, because it answers a question the user actually has. The honest combination is 3's voice applied to 4's structure, which is a bigger piece of work than either alone. 1 is the safe, quick improvement; 2 is really a product decision about whether Deals is a peer of Request or not.
